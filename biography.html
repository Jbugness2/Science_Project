Early Life

Caroline Herschel was born in Hanover, Germany on March 16th, 1750, Although at this time Hanover was a duchy of the Roman Empire. Caroline lived a challenging childhood plagued with little support and a life effecting illness but still grew to be a revered astronomer. She was the 8th of ten children and was supported most by her father Isaac, who was both supportive and intelligent and it was his questions about the world that likely sparked Caroline's interest in astronomy and the natural world. However, Her mother was opposed to the idea of her education expecting her to help around the house to prepare for a life of domestic service. As a result Caroline Herschel received her education from her brother Sir William Herschel. During her childhood she contracted typhus and never grew past 4" 3' but even this hardship didn't dissuade her from pursuing her passion for discovery and exploration.

Issac Herschel was the leading force in Caroline's educational development without Issac Caroline would likely not have become an astronomer that pushed social conventions and made major contributions to astronomical catalogs. When Issac passed in 1767 Caroline's life grew harder as her mother became more persistent in training her as a domestic servant. To make things harder at this time William was already established as an astronomer in England. However, this separation also offered hope to Caroline when William offered to bring her to England, and of course she eagerly accepted the offer.

Move to England

She arrived in England at her brother's house in 1772, Bath, England. Finally life for Caroline became easier as she was learning form her brother who was already well established in many musical communities, This was exactly what Caroline needed so she took vocal lessons and preformed with William. However today we don't know Caroline as the musically talented singer, we know her as the astronomer. William had another passion that Caroline threw herself into, that passion was studying the stars. This growing passion soon took over both of their lives as they became obsessed with discoveries and catalogs. During this time Caroline was acting as assistant to William as she quickly learned everything surrounding this passion. As she continued to learn under William as his assistant her ambition and need to discover grew until she became independent in her pursuit of answers and new questions. She grew to become the person we know, the leader in women astronomy and a dedicated scientist.

My father wished to give me something like a polished education, but my mother was particularly determined that it should be a rough.— Caroline Herschel

Independent Astronomer

When William died in 1822, Caroline now 72 returned to Hanover and continued her work in the place she loved. While their she collected all of the work she and William had done and created an ordered index of their discoveries. She gave this catalogue to a nephew so he could learn from it. During her time she finally received the belated honors due her and was given many awards and invitations to prestigious societies. Caroline later died at the age of 97 in 1848 and will always be remembered for her contributions to astronomy and as a pioneer for women in science.

"Miss Herschel's industry and attention…have been productive of the most important consequences."— Nevil Maskelyne, Astronomer Royal, letter to Caroline Herschel, 1786
